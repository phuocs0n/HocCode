## Writing a Tagline

A tagline is the one line that appears under or next to the product name everywhere. It does the job the name cannot fully do - it fills in the "what is this and why do I care" question in under ten words.

SpeedBlip's tagline: "Know your speed. Before it slows you down."

That line does three things at once. It names the user behavior (know your speed), it creates mild urgency (before it slows you down), and it implies passive monitoring without saying the word "monitoring."

A strong tagline:

- Is under 10 words
- Creates a small emotional hook (urgency, curiosity, relief)
- Does not explain features - it describes the user's outcome
- Reads naturally when spoken aloud

It is good practice to write five taglines before picking one. The first one is usually functional but flat. The third or fourth is usually where the real hook shows up.
