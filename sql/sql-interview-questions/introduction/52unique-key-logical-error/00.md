## What's Wrong With This Code?

```sql
1CREATE TABLE Students (
2  id INT PRIMARY KEY,
3  name VARCHAR(50),
4  email VARCHAR(100) UNIQUE,
5  phone VARCHAR(20) UNIQUE
6);
7
```

Here, the code above is attempting to create two UNIQUE key constraints on the email and phone columns simultaneously, which is valid.

But the problem is that each value in the email and phone columns must be distinct across all rows.

However, it is uncommon for every student to have a unique email and phone number.

As a result, this constraint may lead to conflicts and prevent the insertion of multiple rows with the same email or phone number, which is often necessary for real-world scenarios.

#### How to You Fix It?

To fix the above issue, you may want to reconsider the UNIQUE constraints on the two columns.

You can modify the code according to your requirement.

For example, you might choose to remove the UNIQUE constraint altogether or apply it to a different column that truly requires unique values.

You could also choose to use the UNIQUE constraint with only the email column, as most students indeed have unique emails.
